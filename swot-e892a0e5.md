# After-action SWOT — Her Voice, Not Her Words

## Strengths
- Overwhelming majority chose out-of-band verification — calling or texting Principal Ellison on a known number — before sharing or commenting on the clip, demonstrating strong instincts around the core deepfake defense. _(injects: 0712d7b7…)_
- Near-unanimous selection of 'acknowledge concern, no playback, escalate to admin' when a student asked if the clip was real, showing good judgment about protecting students and keeping verification with those who can act. _(injects: c37e45b9…)_
- Strong consensus on a holding message that named uncertainty honestly ('authenticity is not confirmed; do not share') rather than overclaiming truth or falsehood, modeling sound crisis communications. _(injects: 6fe63ec7…)_
- Participants correctly treated an inconclusive deepfake-detector score as a clue rather than proof, pairing it with continued out-of-band verification instead of over-trusting or weaponizing the tool. _(injects: 316fde9e…)_
- Essentially all participants recognized voice-clone vishing targeting Accounts Payable and chose to hang up and verify on a known district number before moving money — a textbook fraud defense. _(injects: f8269405…)_
- After the reveal, most participants supported Ellison publicly with facts and left suspect identification to admin/law enforcement, avoiding mob dynamics and protecting due process. _(injects: 079e54f3…)_
- Strong forward-looking consensus to embed verification habits and deepfake/vishing awareness into curriculum rather than treating the incident as a one-off PR problem. _(injects: 5de59bb0…)_

## Weaknesses
- A meaningful minority (roughly a third) chose to run the clip through an online deepfake detector as a first step rather than going straight to out-of-band verification, risking delay and false reassurance. _(injects: 0712d7b7…)_
- On the leave-pressure decision, votes were notably split across silence, immediate paid leave, and even a public resignation petition, showing inconsistent instincts about due process under public pressure. _(injects: b41fe0cd…)_
- A small number of participants leaned toward naming the suspected insider in the teacher lounge or recommending the district stay quiet entirely, both of which risk rumor propagation or leaving a cleared target undefended. _(injects: 079e54f3…)_
- Some participants favored 'no comment until lawyers finish reviewing' during the holding-message decision, which protects legal exposure but leaves a communications vacuum that rumor fills quickly.

## Opportunities
- Reinforce with explicit drills why 'send to detector first' is a secondary step, not a substitute for known-channel contact, since a sizable cohort defaulted to the tool. _(injects: 0712d7b7, 316fde9e…)_
- Build a clear, rehearsed decision tree for leave/duty-adjustment recommendations during unverified-media crises so staff converge faster under public pressure. _(injects: b41fe0cd…)_
- Extend finance-side training on voice-clone vishing beyond this exercise, given how uniformly strong the response was here — capture and replicate that training approach elsewhere in the district. _(injects: f8269405…)_
- Use the strong consensus on curriculum integration as a mandate to formalize a recurring (not one-time) verification and deepfake-literacy program for students and staff.

## Threats
- Detector over-reliance remains a live risk: contradictory public detector screenshots show how easily unverified scores can be weaponized by any side of a dispute. _(injects: 316fde9e…)_
- Public and board pressure for immediate punitive action (leave, resignation petitions) before investigation completes threatens due process and could chill future willingness to serve in leadership roles. _(injects: b41fe0cd…)_
- Insider-enabled deepfakes combined with simultaneous vishing attacks show attackers can exploit organizational distraction to hit multiple fronts (reputation and finance) at once, straining incident response capacity. _(injects: f8269405…)_
- Rumor and gossip about suspect identity, even from well-intentioned staff, create legal and reputational liability that can undermine an otherwise well-handled recovery.

## Recommended follow-ups
- Codify a one-page 'verify before you forward' protocol with the known-contact numbers for principals and superintendent, distributed to all staff ahead of the next drill.
- Run a follow-up tabletop focused specifically on the leave/due-process decision point to tighten team consensus under public pressure.
- Partner IT/finance to formalize the voice-call verification step (callback on known number) as mandatory policy for any payment change request, regardless of apparent caller identity.
- Integrate deepfake-detector limitations training so staff understand scores are investigative clues, not forensic proof, reducing future reliance on screenshots as evidence.
- Develop a rumor-control communication template for insider-suspect scenarios that explicitly discourages lounge/informal speculation and funnels concerns to admin/LE.
- Add recurring (not one-time) student and staff modules on AI voice fakes and vishing to the CTE/advisory curriculum, building on this exercise's near-unanimous support.
